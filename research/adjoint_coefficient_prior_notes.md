# Adjoint/coefficient-only residual extraction: primary-source audit

19 November 2026. Focus: forward propagation of a regular solution, extraction of one infinity coefficient using a finite asymptotic test function, and a certified residual tail without solving the outgoing solution backward.

## Closest direct integral identities

A. Kievsky, M. Viviani, P. Barletta, C. Romero-Redondo, E. Garrido, *Variational description of continuum states in terms of integral relations*, [arXiv:1002.3756](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1002.3756), §II, equations (4)–(7). Green's identity gives the scattering coefficients directly as integrals of the physical solution against the differential residual of a reference test function: A=(m/ℏ²)⟨Ψ,(H−E)G⟩ and B=−(m/ℏ²)⟨Ψ,(H−E)F⟩. Equations (18)–(21) connect these to second-order variational accuracy. The paper does not provide the proposed complex-ray, finite-asymptotic-polynomial, explicit infinite-tail bound.

C. W. Johnson, B. M. Loc, A. Keller, K. M. Nollett, *Scattering phase shifts from overlap relations in the J-matrix method*, [arXiv:2412.08825v3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.08825), §III, equations (15)–(19). Explicit Wronskian/integral coefficient extraction and a normalization-independent ratio; continuous coordinate derivation assumes finite-range potential, followed by a discrete-basis analog. It attributes the continuous method to existing scattering-overlap/integral relations and illustrates improved accuracy numerically. This is a close identity-level precedent, not a complex-ray residual tail certificate.

T. Kato, *Upper and Lower Bounds of Scattering Phases*, Progress of Theoretical Physics 6 (1951), 394–407, [DOI 10.1143/ptp/6.3.394](https://doi.org/10.1143/ptp/6.3.394), [author paper scan](https://scispace.com/pdf/upper-and-lower-bounds-of-scattering-phases-2tv3ktvbui.pdf). Section 1, equations (6)–(10), applies Green's formula to obtain an exact output-error identity; §§2–3, especially (23)–(24), bound scattering phase and weighted solution errors by a weighted residual norm and auxiliary spectral bounds. This establishes rigorous residual-based scattering-output certification as an old principle. Its real, self-adjoint variational framework differs materially from complex QNM infinity tails.

## Stokes computation audit

A. B. Olde Daalhuis, F. W. J. Olver, *On the calculation of Stokes multipliers for linear differential equations of the second order*, Methods Appl. Anal. 2 (1995), 348–367, [publisher archive PDF](https://archive.intlpress.com/site/pub/files/_fulltext/journals/maa/1995/0002/0003/MAA-1995-0002-0003-a006.pdf). Section 7 computes multipliers by numerically continuing asymptotically initialized solutions and applying a connection formula, equation (7.1), discussing monotonic-singulant paths and instability. The other principal method uses large-order coefficient asymptotics. This is not the proposed forward regular-solution weighted residual-tail certificate.

M. Loday-Richaud, M. Mezzarobba, P. Remy, *Rigorous numerical computation of the Stokes multipliers for linear differential equations with single level one*, [arXiv:2601.04901v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.04901). The rigorous implemented algorithm works in the Borel plane, reducing local Stokes computation to regular-singular connection problems. It handles arbitrary order and degeneracies, with algebraic polynomial coefficients and ball error tracking. Crucially, the introduction, p.4, explicitly states that moving local Stokes automorphisms to a common global basis remains a connection problem requiring effective summation: their algorithm does not eliminate it. Thus it does not directly subsume the proposed horizon-to-infinity scalar coefficient certificate.

D. A. Lutz, R. Schäfke, *Calculating connection coefficients for meromorphic differential equations*, Complex Variables 34 (1997), 145–170, [DOI 10.1080/17476939708815044](https://doi.org/10.1080/17476939708815044). Only primary abstract inspected: rank-one systems with distinct leading eigenvalues, convergent sums/sequence limits, weighted formal/Frobenius coefficients and singular-integral recurrences. Full text not retrieved, so equivalence to the proposed certificate is unresolved.

## Interpretation

The extraction identity itself is classical Green/Lagrange duality and has direct scattering precedents. General output-weighted residual estimation is likewise established; see also Pierce–Giles, JCP200(2004)769–794, [author PDF](https://people.maths.ox.ac.uk/~gilesm/files/jcp04.pdf), §§2–3. No exact equivalent of the specific CHE/QNM finite-radius data-dependent residual-tail bound was found in the inspected sources. That is a bounded search result, not a novelty proof.

A possible concrete distinction is a computable coefficient-only CHE tail certificate, uniform on a specified complex-parameter chart, using the already propagated horizon state and a finite test polynomial, with no outgoing-state enclosure. If it demonstrably gives materially smaller certified errors near QNMs at lower cost, that is a defensible algorithmic result. It should be described as a specialization and adaptation of classical coefficient-extraction/adjoint-error ideas until a broader novelty audit establishes more.
