# Identification of the lateral germs and the threshold boundary

For an arbitrary fixed magnitude s>0 take
Ω(t)=s exp[i(π/2∓πt)] and θ(t)=±πt, 0≤t≤1. Then Im(Ωe^{iθ})=s, and t=0 lies on the physical upper imaginary frequency axis with the real positive spatial ray. For the upper signs the frequency approaches the cut with a positive real part and the spatial ray traverses the upper half-plane. The lower signs give the left bank. The horizon poles at −in/2 lie away from the open frequency paths. If s=1/2, multiply the horizon solution by 1−2iΩ before taking the endpoint.

Each parameter pair has a product neighborhood on which Im(Ωe^{iθ}) exceeds a positive constant δ. Pick the radius R₀ sufficiently large that |Ω|/(R₀−1)<δ, with a strict margin throughout the closed product neighborhood. The logarithmic derivative of x=z+Log(z−1) changes the derivative of Re(2iΩx) along a radial line by at most 2|Ω|/(R₀−1). The forward Volterra evolution therefore has uniformly bounded kernel on every radial ray of the neighborhood. At the finite common inner arc the analytic continuation of the horizon data and their derivatives is bounded. Uniform integrability of v_s=L/z²+1/z³ on the rays and the forward Volterra estimate give a uniform bound on h in the swept unbounded sector. The exact concomitant satisfies dA_N/dz=h R_N/(2iΩ) there, with R_N=O(|z|^-{N+2}). Consequently the difference between its values at the endpoints of any connecting outer arc of radius T is O(T^(-N-1)). The two ray limits agree. The same proof applies to small parallel translations of the terminal ray (or use bounded-length connectors). This establishes both ray-independence and local holomorphy of the coefficient: for parameters in a strict compact overlap the residual tail tends to zero uniformly and the finite-point data are holomorphic.

A finite chain of these overlaps along t transports the physical coefficient to the specified germ on the cut. From the common exterior point z=3/2, the upper (lower) exterior arc followed by the corresponding shifted negative-direction ray is homotopic to the coded polygon passing above (below) both 0 and 1. One may deform finite paths in the upper (lower) half-plane away from the singular points, carrying Log(z−1) from its real value. The two continuations are complex conjugates on the frequency axis because the coefficients are real and the symmetry is Ω→−conjugate(Ω). In the retained gauge the powers of Log occur only in the definition of the physical phases; their continuation fixes the sheets.

For the threshold monopole, Im Ω=0 on the real-frequency diameter removes strict damping along a negative-real spatial ray. Extend the branch from Im Ω<0 by continuity using |K_Ω(d)|≤|d|≤t−s and ∫_R^∞(t−R)t^(-3)dt=1/(2R). Each Volterra iterate is continuous up to the boundary and the factorial majorant gives locally uniform summability there. This extension reproduces the real scattering coefficient by continuity on its physical bank.

The static Legendre Wronskian has the stated sign. The dimensionless four-dimensional radial variable gives u_H^0=zP_l(2z−1), u_b^0=2(2l+1)!/(l!)² zQ_l(2z−1), W_x=−(2l+1)!/(l!)². For l=0 the global static field Phi=const corresponds to an unbounded radial master u~z at infinity. At Ω=−i/2, (1−2iΩ)h_H has leading coefficient (1+L)(z−1), a nonzero regularized solution with the analytic path-determined horizon line. Its nonvanishing lateral Wronskians entail simple poles of the individual unit-horizon incoming amplitudes. These normalization poles cancel in the Green function expression u_Hu_out/W.
